> During World War II, physicists on the Manhattan Project worried that the Nazi German nuclear program might achieve the first nuclear bomb before the US and its allies. That’s not what transpired, but historians have debated just how close the Germans came to achieving criticality in their last attempt at building a reactor: the B8 pile. Werner Heisenberg, who led that effort, claimed that they were close and had only needed a little more uranium. But according to [a paper](https://academic.oup.com/pnasnexus/article-lookup/doi/10.1093/pnasnexus/pgag282) published in the Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences Nexus, that was not the case: The project simply didn’t have sufficient uranium and heavy water to build a working bomb, given the design they were using.



在第二次世界大战期间，Manhattan Project 的物理学家们担心纳粹德国的核计划可能会在美国及其盟友之前制造出第一颗原子弹。但事实并非如此，历史学家们一直在争论，德国人在他们最后一次尝试建造反应堆—— B8 pile ——时，距离达到临界状态究竟有多近。领导这项工作的 Werner Heisenberg 声称，他们当时已经非常接近，只需要再多一点铀。但根据发表在《Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences Nexus》上的[一篇论文](https://academic.oup.com/pnasnexus/article-lookup/doi/10.1093/pnasnexus/pgag282)，情况并非如此：鉴于他们当时所采用的设计，该项目根本没有足够的铀和重水来制造一颗能正常工作的原子弹。

> This particular research started in 2013, when University of Maryland physicist Timothy Koeth—a co-author on the new paper—received the gift of a mysterious heavy metal cube from a friend. He [instantly recognized](https://www.eurekalert.org/pub_releases/2019-05/aiop-sfl042619.php) it as one of the uranium cubes used by German scientists in their unsuccessful attempt to build a working nuclear reactor. Only 14 such cubes are known to exist today.

这项研究始于 2013 年，当时美国马里兰大学物理学家 Timothy Koeth——本论文的合著者之一——从朋友那里收到一个神秘的重金属立方体作为礼物。他 [立即认出](https://www.eurekalert.org/pub_releases/2019-05/aiop-sfl042619.php) 它是德国科学家在未能建造正常工作的核反应堆的尝试中使用的铀立方体之一。目前已知仅存 14 个此类立方体。

> In 2019, [we reported](https://arstechnica.com/science/2019/06/physicists-hunt-uranium-cubes-to-shed-light-on-germanys-failed-nuclear-reactor/) on Koeth’s quest to track down the cube’s origins, as well as several other similar cubes that had somehow found their way across the Atlantic. At the time, Koeth and his partner in the quest, then-graduate student Miriam “Mimi” Hiebert, had tracked down 10 such cubes around the US. For instance, the Smithsonian Institute had a German uranium cube in storage. They tracked a third cube to Harvard University, where it was regularly passed around to students in introductory physics classes as a curiosity.

在 2019 年， [我们报道](https://arstechnica.com/science/2019/06/physicists-hunt-uranium-cubes-to-shed-light-on-germanys-failed-nuclear-reactor/) 关于 Koeth 追踪立方体起源的行动，以及几个类似的立方体 somehow 通过大西洋找到美国。当时，Koeth 与这项追踪的合作伙伴—— 当时的研究生 Miriam “Mimi” Hiebert—— 已经在美利坚合众国找到了 10 个此类立方体。例如，史密森尼学会（Smithsonian Institute）的仓库中存放着一枚德国 Uranium cube。他们将第三枚立方体追踪到哈佛大学， 在那里它经常被分发给入门物理课学生作为一种好奇的事物。

> Underpinning the Manhattan Project in the US was the fear that German scientists under Adolf Hitler’s Nazi regime would [beat the Allies](https://www.atomicheritage.org/history/german-atomic-bomb-project) to a nuclear bomb. The Germans had [a two-year head start](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_nuclear_weapons_program), but according to Koeth, “fierce competition over finite resources, bitter interpersonal rivalries, and ineffectual scientific management” resulted in significant delays in their progress toward achieving a sustained nuclear reaction. German nuclear scientists were separated into three isolated groups based in Berlin (B), Gottow (G), and Leipzig (L).

为了推动美国的曼哈顿计划，背后有一个重要的动因：人们担心在希特勒纳粹政权统治下的德国科学家会比同盟国更早地制造出核弹。德国人比同盟国早了两年，但据科斯所言，由于“对有限资源的激烈竞争、苦涩的人际关系和无效的科学管理”，他们的持续核反应进展出现了重大延误。德国核科学家被分为三个独立的组别，分别位于柏林（B）、戈托（G）和莱比锡（L）。

> [Heisenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Werner_Heisenberg) headed the Berlin group, and as the Allied forces advanced in the winter of 1944, Heisenberg moved his team to a cave under a castle in a small town called Haigerloch—now the site of the [Atomkeller Museum](https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/atomkeller-museum). That’s where the group built the B8 reactor. It resembled an “ominous chandelier,” per Koeth, because it was composed of 664 uranium cubes strung together with aircraft cable and then submerged in a tank of heavy water shielded by graphite to prevent radiation exposure.

[Heisenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Werner_Heisenberg) 率领柏林小组，随着盟军在1944 年冬季的推进，Heisenberg 将他的团队移至一座城堡下的洞穴中，位于小镇 Haigerloch，现在的[Atomkeller Museum](https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/atomkeller-museum) 所在地。那里是该组建造了B8 反应堆。它看起来像一座“阴森的吊灯”，据 科埃思称，因为它由664 颗铀块用飞机线串联而成，随后浸入被石墨屏蔽的重水罐中，以防止辐射暴露。

> As the German scientists were racing against time, Manhattan Project lead [Lieutenant General Leslie Groves](https://www.atomicheritage.org/profile/leslie-r-groves) kicked off a covert mission dubbed “Alsos,” with the express purpose of gathering information and materials related to Germany’s scientific research. When Allied forces closed in, Heisenberg took apart the B8 experiment and buried the uranium cubes in a field, ferreting away key documentation in a latrine. (Pity [Samuel Goudsmit](https://www.atomicheritage.org/profile/samuel-goudsmit), the poor physicist who had to dig those out.) Heisenberg escaped by bicycle, carrying a few cubes in a backpack.

当德国科学家们与时间赛跑时，曼哈顿计划负责人莱斯利·格罗夫斯中将发起了一项代号为“阿洛斯”的秘密行动，目的是收集与德国科学研究相关的信息和资料。面对盟军逼近，海森堡拆除了B8实验装置，将铀立方体埋在田野中，并将关键文件藏匿在厕所内。（可怜的Samuel Goudsmit，这位物理学家不得不挖出这些文件。）海森堡骑着自行车逃脱，背包里装了几个铀立方体。

> ## A critical reassessment

## 一个关键的重新评估

> As Heisenberg reported, the German scientists’ final experiment failed because the amount of uranium in the cubes was insufficient to trigger a sustained nuclear reaction. But Heisenberg was confident that “a slight increase in its size would have been sufficient to start off the process of energy production.” A model [described in a 2009 paper](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00016-008-0396-0) supported that, showing the group would have needed only 50 percent more uranium cubes to make the design work.

根据海森堡的报告，德国科学家的最终实验失败是因为立方体中的铀量不足以引发持续的核反应。但海森堡确信，"稍微增加其尺寸就足以启动能量生产过程。" 一项模型[在2009年的论文中描述](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00016-008-0396-0) 支持了这一点，表明该团队只需要多用50%的铀立方体就能让设计奏效。

> Koeth and Hiebert uncovered a box of declassified documents about German uranium in the National Archives and found about 400 other uranium cubes from a separate reactor experiment by the Gottow group. They concluded that the combined inventory would have been more than enough to achieve criticality in the B8 reactor, suggesting that Germany’s secretive, isolationist approach hampered their nuclear program, because the two groups weren’t sharing information or resources.

Koeth 和 Hiebert 在国家档案馆发现了一箱关于德国铀的解密文件，并从 Gottow 研究组的另一个反应堆实验中找到了约 400 个其他铀立方体。他们得出结论，合并后的库存量足以让 B8 反应堆达到临界状态，这表明德国那种秘而不宣、各自为政的方式阻碍了其核计划，因为这两个研究组既不共享信息，也不共享资源。

> This latest paper builds on that earlier work but reaches a different conclusion. Koeth, Hiebert, and their co-authors analyzed two of the surviving uranium cubes, along with combing through historical records, technical reports, correspondence, and similar archival sources to learn more about the uranium, heavy water, and graphite blocks used for the B8 reactor experiment.

这篇最新论文基于那项早期工作，但得出了不同的结论。Koeth、Hiebert 和他们的合著者分析了两块幸存的铀方块，同时查阅了历史记录、技术报告、往来信件和其他类似的档案来源，以了解更多关于用于 B8 反应堆实验的铀、重水和石墨块的信息。

> Among their findings: The B8 reactor would have needed between 1,292 and 1,775 uranium cubes to achieve criticality, and twice the heavy water produced by the Norsk Hydro plant in Vemork, Norway—the only source at the time—over the course of the entire war. In short, “Our calculations suggest Heisenberg significantly underestimated the materials required,” the authors wrote.

他们的发现包括：B8 反应堆若要达到临界，需要 1,292 到 1,775 个铀立方体，以及在整个战争期间，需要两倍于挪威 Vemork, Norway 地区 Norsk Hydro 工厂生产的重水——当时这是唯一的来源。简而言之，作者写道：“我们的计算表明，Heisenberg 显著低估了所需材料。”

> The authors also cited prior work noting key differences in American and German graphite production. The former used petroleum coke. While some of this supply reached Germany, the British naval blockade in January 1940 deprived the Germans of that vital resource. The German nuclear physicists had to use metallurgical coke from the Ruhr, which had twice as many impurities as American coke. (The US scientists had similar issues until they located an unusually low-boron coke in Pennsylvania.) Thus, “no natural uranium, graphite-moderated pile could have gone critical” with the materials available to the German scientists at the time.

作者还引用了先前的研究，指出了美国和德国石墨生产的关键差异。前者使用石油焦。虽然部分这种供应到达了德国，但 1940 年 1 月的英国海军封锁切断了德国获取这一关键资源的途径。德国核物理学家不得不使用鲁尔地区的冶金焦，其杂质含量是美国焦炭的两倍。（美国科学家也面临类似问题，直到他们在宾夕法尼亚州找到了一种异常低硼的焦炭。）因此，用当时德国科学家可用的材料，“任何天然铀、石墨慢化堆都不可能达到临界状态”。
