> America has lost more than 200k jobs in “creative” industries over the last four years, with roughly 50k lost within the last year alone. That makes for one of the worst stretches for media employment in modern US history, with similar job loss intensity and duration occurring only during the major economic recessions of 2001 and 2008. Yet there’s no generalized recession today—instead, this period of job loss coincides with the rise of AI systems that can compose wholesale novels, photorealistic images, soundalike music, and practically every other form of digital art, en masse and at extremely low costs. Is this the fall of the Creative Class?

# 美国在过去四年中，创意产业失去了超过20万个工作岗位，仅在过去一年就失去了大约5万个。这使得这成为现代美国历史上媒体就业的最糟糕时期之一，类似的失业强度和持续时间仅在2001年和2008年的重大经济衰退期间出现。然而，今天并没有大规模的经济衰退——相反，这一失业时期与能够创作整本小说、照片级真实的图像、听起来像音乐的声音以及几乎所有其他形式的数字艺术的AI系统的兴起同时发生。这是创意阶层的衰落吗？

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!4jWS!,w_1456,c_limit,f_webp,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F21e0c6b5-22e0-4e51-97b0-68bb9abe225a_2886x1843.png)

> Proving the exact amount of AI-driven job loss in the arts is extremely difficult. Media firms are decidedly coy about their AI use, both to protect against public backlash and, more financially important, to preserve the legal basis for their copyrights. The effects of AI are also hard to disentangle from other factors currently affecting arts businesses, like consolidation in Hollywood, offshoring of content production, or the continued displacement of traditional media providers in favor of social media creators. Yet perhaps the clearest evidence of AI’s influence is just that all subsectors of the digital arts industry are losing jobs, while in-person entertainment is still growing at a healthy pace.

证明艺术领域 AI 驱动的岗位流失的确切数量极其困难。媒体公司对其 AI 使用极为谨慎，既是为了规避公众反弹，更是为了保护其版权的法律基础。AI 的影响同样难以从目前影响艺术业务的其他因素中剥离，如 Hollywood 的整合、内容制作的外包，或传统媒体提供商被社交媒体创作者取代的持续趋势。然而，或许 AI 的影响力最明确的证据就是所有数字艺术子行业都在流失岗位，而现场娱乐仍以健康的速度增长。

> Some of those digital job losses are just a continuation of prior trends, like in the publishing industry where technological change has been continually grinding away newspaper and magazine jobs for decades. Yet for sectors like live broadcasting, streaming, or graphic design, recent experience is an unusual downturn compared to the tranquility of years prior. Then there’s the worst-hit sector, movie & sound recording, which has been bleeding jobs at a nearly unprecedented pace over the last three years.

其中一些数字化就业损失只是延续了以往的趋势，例如在出版行业，技术变革几十年来持续削减着报纸和杂志的工作岗位。然而，对于现场广播、流媒体或图形设计等行业而言，最近的衰退与过去几年的稳定时期相比显得异常。从最受影响的电影和录音制作行业来看，在过去三年中，几乎以前所未有的速度流失着工作岗位。

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Exun!,w_1456,c_limit,f_webp,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fdecb86b5-a38f-46ea-bce2-c6d5d1a405d5_2886x1843.png)

> Hollywood has never seen a stretch as bad as the last four years, with the movie & TV industry losing more than 100k jobs, nearly one-third of the sector’s total. At the depths touched this summer, total employment was lower than at any point since the 2008 recession and approaching the lowest point in 30 years. The streaming era has proven an extremely difficult transition, with traditional films and TV shows losing watch time to user-generated and increasingly AI-assisted or AI-generated content flows.

好莱坞过去四年所经历的衰退前所未有，其电影和电视产业损失了超过10万个就业岗位，几乎占到该行业总就业人数的三分之一。在今年夏天的低点，总就业人数低于2008年金融危机以来的最低点，并逼近近30年来的最低水平。流媒体时代的转型证明极其艰难，传统电影和电视节目在观看时间上逐渐让位于用户生成内容，以及日益依赖人工智能辅助或由人工智能生成的内容流。

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!zm0D!,w_1456,c_limit,f_webp,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F80d207ac-eea2-417b-a381-f956e19ede9d_2886x1843.png)

> Overall, nearly half the media job losses of the last four years have been concentrated in the movie & sound recording sector that includes Hollywood. Written publishing has been the next-largest source of job loss, with employment down by more than 70k over the same time frame. Yet the job losses have by no means been contained to any particular part of media; instead they’ve hit nearly every subsector at some point. Nor do they show any sign of abating, with losses consistently hovering at around 50k per year and even accelerating in recent months. Will further AI development continue displacing workers in creative industries?

总体而言，过去四年里近一半的媒体职位裁员集中在电影和音频录制行业（ Hollywood）。书面出版是接下来最大的裁员来源，在此期间就业下降超过 70k。然而，这些裁员并未局限于媒体的任何特定部分；相反，它们在某个时点影响了几乎所有子行业。而且，裁员态势似乎没有减缓，损失每年保持在约 50k，甚至在最近几个月加速了。进一步的 AI 发展将继续导致创意行业的工人被替代吗？

> ## Can the Arts Business Model Survive?

# 艺术产业的商业模式能够生存下去吗？

> To understand what **could** happen to the business of art amidst the rise of AI, it’s important to understand what **did** happen to it during the first digital transition, the rise of the internet.

要理解在 AI 兴起之际艺术商业**可能**会发生什么，了解在第一次数字化转型——即互联网的兴起——期间它**确实**发生了什么，这一点至关重要。

> It was the turn of the millennium, and *Metallica* had a problem. Demos of their upcoming, unreleased tracks were bouncing around radio and the internet, originally leaked onto a new internet file-sharing website called Napster. There they found thousands of files shared amongst hundreds of thousands of users, many of which were direct rips of albums that sold for $15 being offered for free. The most anticipated albums were often leaked online even before they were on store shelves. *Metallica* sued Napster for copyright infringement, and they were soon joined by separate suits from other prominent artists and eventually the Recording Industry Association of America (RIAA).



那是在千禧年交替之际，*Metallica* 遇到了一个难题。他们即将发行但尚未正式发布的曲目小样在广播和互联网上广泛传播，最初是泄露到了一个名为 Napster 的新型互联网文件共享网站上。在那里，成千上万个文件在数十万用户之间共享，其中许多是直接盗录的、售价 $15 的专辑，却被免费提供。最受期待的专辑甚至在上架销售之前就经常在网上泄露。*Metallica* 因版权侵权起诉了 Napster，随后其他知名艺术家也纷纷提起单独诉讼，最终美国唱片业协会 (RIAA) 也加入了起诉。

> Much of the general public was understandably unsympathetic to *Metallica*, perceived as a bunch of already-successful millionaires trying to bilk even more money from listeners, and were even less sympathetic towards the RIAA, perceived as scurrilous middlemen who take from artists and fans alike. But the two of them easily won their lawsuit, forcing Napster into bankruptcy. Yet *Metallica* may have been right on the law but were on the wrong side of technology; the RIAA had won the battle but was losing the war.

相当多的普通公众出于可以理解的理由对 *Metallica* 缺乏同情，他们被视为一群已经成功的百万富翁，试图从听众身上榨取更多金钱；而且他们对 RIAA 的同情程度甚至更低，后者被视为专横且可鄙的中介，同样从艺术家和粉丝那里拿走钱。但他们俩轻松赢得了诉讼，迫使 Napster 进入破产。然而 *Metallica* 可能在法律上是正确的，但他们在技术的错误一边；RIAA 赢了这场战斗，但正在失去战争。

> The modern internet made file-sharing extremely easy, and no matter how much whac-a-mole companies played, they could not possibly catch every illegal upload. The perennial threat of piracy undermined their copyright and limited their ability to charge for music. Eventually, the value of individual songs fell to the point that companies like Spotify and Apple Music could swoop in to acquire massive catalogues, consolidate them, and charge a comparatively trivial fee for access. For what a single *Metallica* album would have cost in 2000, you can now get a month’s worth of unlimited access to nearly all human-made music.

现代互联网使文件共享变得极其容易，无论公司如何玩“打地鼠”游戏，都不可能抓住每一个非法上传者。长期存在的盗版威胁动摇了他们的版权根基，并限制了他们对音乐收费的能力。最终，单曲的价值跌落至谷底，Spotify 和 Apple Music 等公司得以乘虚而入，收购庞大的曲库，整合资源，并以相对微不足道的费用提供访问权限。用 2000 年购买一张 Metallica 专辑的钱，现在可以买到一个月近乎所有人类创作音乐的无限畅听权。

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!ItXZ!,w_1456,c_limit,f_webp,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fa9937364-33e6-4ce6-a504-e91f8a02c625_2886x1843.png)

> The music industry has never returned to the heyday paydays of the peak CD era—even without adjusting for inflation, streaming revenue pales in comparison to ‘90s physical media sales. This was a massive boon to consumers, but it was also a squeeze on musicians that forced them to fundamentally change their business model over time. Instead of just selling records to earn money directly, music itself increasingly became a loss-leading advertisement for the live concerts (and merch) that provided a growing share of artists’ income. Tours got longer, venues got bigger, ticket prices skyrocketed, and musicians frequently took on roles closer to public influencers than isolated artistes. Were it not for rising concert sales, the business model of music would have completely collapsed, and the income going to musicians would have cratered.

音乐产业从未回归过 CD 时代巅峰时的高峰收益， streaming revenue 也远不及 '90s physical media sales。 这对消费者是巨大的福利，但对音乐人来说却是一股挤压力，迫使他们不得不随时间根本改变商业模式。 除了直接销售唱片赚钱，音乐本身越来越成为 loss-leading advertisement for live concerts (and merch) that provided a growing share of artists’ income。 巡演变长，场馆变大，票价飙升，音乐人经常扮演角色更接近 public influencers 而非 isolated artistes。 如果不是因为巡演销量的上升，音乐产业的商业模式本应完全崩溃，音乐人获得的收入也将暴跌。

> Of course, there were still significant downsides to the transition into the streaming era. Bands (especially smaller ones) complain about the unending pressure to always be on tour. Musicians whose content was suited to home listening lost out to those more suited to giant festivals. Even accounting for concert revenues, musicians made less money than before. Yet musicians fared better than many areas of entertainment subsectors because there was a live component to fall back on—for many media industries, the digital era left no such comfort.

当然，转向流媒体时代仍然带来了显著的弊端。特别是小型乐队，他们抱怨始终处于巡演的不断压力之中。那些适合家庭聆听的内容的音乐家，输给了更适合大型音乐节的音乐家。即便算上演唱会收入，音乐家的收入也低于以前。然而，音乐家比娱乐产业的许多细分领域过得好，因为现场表演是他们可以依靠的组成部分——对于许多媒体产业来说，数字时代没有这样的慰藉。

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!iTEJ!,w_1456,c_limit,f_webp,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F11cbc8aa-b1c1-44c0-80b1-6ef5c763924d_2886x1843.png)

> Over time, the business of video has moved in the opposite direction of music—out of the theatre and into the home. Hollywood formerly made most of its money enticing customers to visit sold-out movie theatres, but these were gradually supplanted by broadcast TV, cable, and physical media. Yet because each successful technological leap increased total video watch time, the industry was still able to thrive amidst technological upheaval. That is, until the modern streaming era.

随着时间的推移，视频业务的发展方向与音乐行业完全相反：从电影院走向家庭。好莱坞过去靠吸引顾客来观看电影院的场次获利丰厚，但逐渐被广播电视、有线电视和实体媒体所取代。然而，每次成功的技术革新都增加了人们观看视频的总时长，因此整个行业仍然能够在技术变革中蓬勃发展。直到现代流媒体时代的到来，一切都发生了改变。

> Today, the plethora of video options and the rise of social media have sent video producers into a vicious competition for limited attention. Even before adjusting for inflation, movie ticket sales ended last year down 25% from their 2019 peak, cable revenue is down 18% from its peak, and streaming revenue has not been able to compensate for the drop. The mountain of free user-generated content on YouTube, Twitch, TikTok, or social media was already presenting harsh competition for traditional video companies in the years before ChatGPT’s launch, and now streaming services are also competing with a flood of AI-generated content. The business model of video media is fundamentally getting squeezed, and unlike in the music industry, there’s basically no equivalent to live concerts that Hollywood can be used to ease the pain.

如今，视频选项的泛滥与社交媒体的崛起，让视频制作陷入了对有限注意力的恶性竞争。即使不考虑通货膨胀因素，去年电影票房收入较 2019 年峰值下降 25%，有线电视收入较峰值下降 18%，而流媒体收入无法弥补这一跌幅。在 ChatGPT 发布前几年，YouTube、Twitch、TikTok 等平台上海量的免费用户生成内容（UGC）已给传统视频公司带来严酷挑战，而现在流媒体服务还要面对 AI 生成内容的洪流冲击。视频媒体的商业模式从根本上正被挤压，与音乐行业不同，好莱坞基本没有类似现场演唱会的替代业务来缓解这种痛苦。

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!dUVd!,w_1456,c_limit,f_webp,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F6c82b577-3785-46e8-935e-246f08d65784_2886x1843.png)

> The actual worst-case scenario for creative workers amidst the AI revolution is something similar to what happened to the publishing industry after the advent of the internet—that is, near-total collapse of their fundamental business model. Newspapers used to employ roughly half a million people in the US, more than the entire oil industry, and now their payrolls are down nearly 85% and still dropping. Magazine publishers likewise have let 66% of their staff go since the 90s, while book publishers have lost 40%.

AI 革命对创意行业从业者而言的实际最坏情况，类似于互联网诞生后出版业遭遇的境况——即他们根本商业模式的几乎全面崩溃。在美国，过去报纸行业雇佣约有半百万人，这超过了整个石油行业的规模；而如今他们的人员支出已下降近 85%，且仍在持续下降。杂志出版商则自 90 年代以来裁撤了 66% 的员工，书籍出版商则损失了 40%。

> The routine informational updates that previously formed newspapers’ bread-and-butter were all de-bundled—box scores moved to sports websites, stock movements went to financial websites, forecasts went to weather websites—each loss compounding on itself to undermine consumers’ need to buy the paper. Search engines became the first place people looked to for information, and newspapers’ advertising revenue rapidly started flowing to companies like Google instead. The collective of social media users became faster at breaking any news story than a daily paper could ever hope to be. When papers eventually did start aggressively paywalling content, the internet made it trivially easy for people to just copy the articles’ content and share it beyond the paywall. The entire business model had collapsed, to the point that now only a select few major newspapers can even survive.

过去构成报纸核心业务的常规资讯更新被全面拆分——球赛比分转移到了体育网站，股市行情流向了财经网站，天气预报归属气象网站——每一项流失相互叠加，共同动摇了读者购买报纸的必要性。搜索引擎成为人们获取信息的首选入口，报纸的广告收入迅速转流向 Google 等公司。社交媒体用户群体在突发新闻速度上，远非日报所能企及。当报纸最终开始大规模设置付费墙时，互联网却让人们轻而易举地复制文章内容并在付费墙外传播。整个商业模式彻底崩塌，以至于现在只有极少数大报能够勉强生存。

> That scale of copyright dilution and forced unbundling is the worst-case scenario for media businesses in the age of AI. It’s possible arts jobs could survive via consumers’ deep-seated aversion to explicitly AI-generated content, but AI is increasingly being used throughout media in ways invisible to the average end consumer. [Roughly 32% of workers in the overall arts, entertainment, and recreation sector use Generative AI to some extent](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/graph/?g=1Z2RG), which is [less than the 62% average across all industries](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/graph/?g=1XQ3Q), but still enough that virtually every major media project could have some AI within its workflow.

那种规模的版权稀释和强制拆分，是 AI 时代媒体企业的最坏情况。有可能艺术岗位能够依靠消费者对明确 AI 生成内容的深层反感而幸存，但 AI 正越来越多地以普通最终消费者不可见的方式贯穿整个媒体行业使用。[总体艺术、娱乐和休闲行业中大约有 32% 的员工以一定程度使用 Generative AI](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/graph/?g=1Z2RG)，这[低于所有行业 62% 的平均水平](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/graph/?g=1XQ3Q)，但仍足以使几乎每个重大媒体项目的工作流程中都包含一些 AI。

> Indeed, AI is likely seeping into media production processes in ways large companies themselves would struggle to prevent even when they desire to—how can a TV studio be sure nobody in their writers’ room is consulting ChatGPT for jokes or any storyboard artist is generating concept art? And even if professional TV and movie studios do effectively hold out against temptation and prevent internal AI use, how long can they compete with the large mass of wannabe independent creators with much fewer scruples? Plenty of media businesses tried to hold out against the algorithmic content waves of the 2010s and were buried as a result.

事实上，AI 很可能正以一种连大公司都难以阻止的方式渗透到媒体制作流程中。即使大公司希望阻止，也无法做到——一个电视工作室如何能确保其编剧室里没有人使用 ChatGPT 来搜集笑话，或者 storyboard 艺术家没有使用 AI 生成概念艺术？即使专业的电视和电影工作室确实抵制住了诱惑并防止了内部使用 AI，他们又能与大量良心更低的独立创作者竞争多久？许多媒体企业曾试图抵制 2010 年代的算法内容浪潮，结果却被淘汰。

> ## Conclusions

## 结论

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Hsfq!,w_1456,c_limit,f_webp,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F9f5df059-4f98-46d1-8a74-e7f94c0aa52e_2886x1843.png)

> Yet fundamentally, it’s difficult to meaningfully predict how new technologies will interact with existing arts businesses. Take the unexpected resurgence in book sales over the last four years. The novel as a commercial art form had been struggling for decades, with fewer books sold in 2019 than at the beginning of the millennium, but the last six years have seen an unprecedented 70% surge in sales. The unlikely savior of the printed word came from perhaps its polar opposite—TikTok.

然而，从根本上说，很难有意义地预测新技术将如何与现有的艺术业务相互作用。以过去四年书籍销售的意外反弹为例。小说作为一种商业艺术形式已经挣扎了几十年，2019年比新千年初的销量更少，但过去六年出现了前所未有的70%的销量增长。印刷文字的意外救星或许来自其对立面——TikTok。

> Of course, fewer people read now than ever before—for the general public, short-form video apps have accelerated the displacement of reading, just as TV, video games, or social media before them. Yet for a dwindling number of dedicated repeat readers, “BookTok” became a new way to source reviews, analysis, discourse, and community in a way that boosted the reading experience and absolutely turbocharged book sales. It’s possible that AI tools could have those same totally unpredictable spillovers for other museums, concerts, or other forms of art in ways that are unpredictable today, in much the same way short-form video saved books in ways that could never have been anticipated beforehand.

当然，现在阅读的人比以往任何时候都要少——对普通大众而言，短视频应用程序加速了阅读的取代，正如电视、视频游戏或社交媒体之前那样。然而，对于逐渐减少的少数忠实重复读者来说，“BookTok”成为了一个全新的方式来获取评论、分析、讨论和社区，从而提升了阅读体验并绝对地推动了图书销售。有可能的是，AI 工具可能在博物馆、音乐会或其他艺术形式中产生出同样不可预料的溢出效应，与短视频意外拯救书籍的方式非常相似，这些效果在此之前是绝对无法预料的。

> At the end of the day, the arts are only a small sector of the overall macroeconomy. Media made up a small share of the labor market even at its peak, and it has always been difficult to get hired in creative industries to the point that making fun of art students’ job prospects became an easy punchline. The movie industry has an income profile closer to eggs or bicycles than something major like healthcare or finance. Were the goal to create art for art’s sake, for the intangible and nonmonetizable cultural benefits, it would be relatively easy to counterbalance any economic impacts of AI on artists just by reversing recent cuts to the National Endowments for the Arts & Humanities.

归根结底，艺术仅占整体宏观经济的一小部分。即使在巅峰时期，媒体行业在劳动力市场中的占比也很小，且向来难以在创意产业获得受聘机会，以至于嘲笑艺术专业学生的就业前景成了现成的笑料。电影行业的收入结构更接近鸡蛋或自行车等行业，而非医疗或金融等支柱产业。若创作艺术的目标纯粹是为艺术而艺术，为了那些无形且不可货币化的文化效益，那么要抵消 AI 对艺术家造成的经济冲击，只需扭转近期对国家艺术与人文基金会预算的削减即可，这相对容易实现。

> It’s telling that Anthropic, the most prominent AI lab, has basically never bothered with image, video, or music generation, because the prospective economic benefits are marginal when compared to using AI to tackle math or scientific problems. OpenAI learned this lesson the hard way when it was forced to abandon its video generation platform Sora, deciding it had better use for its limited compute than making synthetic TikToks. Nowadays, the art generation is done primarily by secondary players running smaller models, though even these are large enough to threaten significant disruption to legacy media businesses.

很值得注意的是，Anthropic 这个最具影响力的 AI 实验室基本上从未涉及过图像、视频或音乐生成，因为与使用 AI 解决数学或科学问题相比，潜在的经济利益微乎其微。
OpenAI 艰苦地学习了这一课，当时它被迫放弃了自己的视频生成平台 Sora，决定将有限的 compute 用于更有价值的事情，而不是制作合成 TikToks。
如今，艺术生成主要由运行较小模型的次级玩家来完成，尽管即使这些模型也足够强大，可能对传统媒体业务造成重大冲击。

> In other words, the primary impacts of AI art will be on culture and society rather than the macroeconomy. Yet arts jobs might be a microcosm for other “creative-adjacent” work that occupies a much larger share of economic activity. Take the tech sector itself, which is the clearest place where AI-driven job loss has shown up outside the media business, as powerful coding agents replace human programmers. Annual tech job gains stood at nearly 300k in 2022, but the sector has been bleeding jobs every year since then in what is now its longest labor market downturn.

换言之，AI 艺术对文化和社会的影响将是主要影响，而非宏观经济。然而，艺术工作可能成为其他“创造相邻”工作的一个微观缩影，而这些工作占据了经济活动中远大的比重。以科技行业本身为例，它是 AI 驱动就业损失在媒体商业之外最明显的领域，因为强大的编码智能体正在取代人类程序员。2022 年，该行业的年度科技工作增长几乎为 30 万，但自那时以来，该行业每年都在裁减工作岗位，如今已进入其历史最长劳动力市场低迷期。

> Right now, AI-driven job displacement has been relatively minor outside of these few narrow sectors. [Unemployment is higher than it was four years ago but still below even the 2010s average](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/graph/?g=1Ya2U), and overall [job turnover rates remain extremely low](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/graph/?g=1Z2Re). Some white-collar sectors that looked prime for AI automation have even been on a hiring spree over the last few years, including [legal](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/graph/?g=1Z2QS) and [engineering](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/graph/?g=1Z2R3) firms. Yet if the economic impact of AI ends up being anywhere near large enough to validate the [trillions of dollars investors are currently betting on it](https://www.apricitas.io/p/americas-1t-ai-gamble), these economic disruptions will only spread from here.

目前，AI 驱动的岗位替代在现正相对平缓，仅限于这些少数几个狭窄的行业。[失业率高于四年前，但仍低于2010年代平均水平](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/graph/?g=1Ya2U)，整体而言[岗位流动率极低](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/graph/?g=1Z2Re)。一些原本被视为适合AI自动化的白领行业，在过去几年甚至处于招聘热潮，其中包括[法律]和[工程]公司。然而，如果AI的经济影响最终达到足以验证投资者[trillions of dollars investors are currently betting on it]的程度，这些经济冲击将只会从此蔓延。
